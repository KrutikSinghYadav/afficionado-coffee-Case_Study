# Executive Summary: Sales Trend and Time-Based Performance Analysis
## Prepared for: Executive Stakeholders & Retail Operations Management
### Date: June 7, 2026
### Dataset Analysis Period: January 1, 2025 – June 30, 2025

---

### 1. Purpose & Scope
This brief outlines the results of a 6-month operational audit of Afficionado Coffee Roasters' retail footprint across three key urban locations: **Astoria**, **Hell's Kitchen**, and **Lower Manhattan**. By analyzing a transaction-level dataset of 149,116 items, we isolate specific time-based demand spikes, identify inefficiencies in current labor allocation, and establish recommendations to optimize payroll overhead and enhance service quality.

---

### 2. High-Level Performance Metrics
Between January and June 2025, the retail network generated a total of **$698,812.33** in revenue across **149,116 line items**. Performance was remarkably stable and balanced across the network:

*   **Hell's Kitchen**: **$236,511.17** (50,735 orders, $4.66 Average Order Value)
*   **Astoria**: **$232,243.91** (50,599 orders, $4.59 Average Order Value)
*   **Lower Manhattan**: **$230,057.25** (47,782 orders, $4.81 Average Order Value)

---

### 3. Core Insights

#### A. The Morning Rush Bottleneck
Customer traffic is heavily concentrated in the morning hours, with **10:00 AM** serving as the universal network peak:
*   **Hell's Kitchen** peaks at 10:00 AM with **6,957 unique transactions** recorded.
*   **Lower Manhattan** experiences earlier activity, with a sharp commute-driven spike starting at **7:00 AM** and peaking at 10:00 AM (**6,297 transactions**).
*   **Astoria** builds more gradually, peaking at 10:00 AM (**5,291 transactions**) and maintaining stable traffic until 12:00 PM.

#### B. The Afternoon Lull
Across all three locations, consumer demand falls by approximately **60%** after **2:00 PM (14:00)**. Continuing to schedule multiple baristas during late afternoon shifts creates significant labor inefficiencies.

#### C. Weekday vs. Weekend Purchase Patterns
*   **Weekdays**: Dominated by high-frequency, single-beverage commuter purchases (low average items per order).
*   **Weekends**: Characterized by larger, group-based transaction sizes, with a higher purchase volume of **Bakery items**, **Teas**, and retail **Coffee Beans**.

---

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

#### I. Labor Re-Alignment (Scheduling Optimization)
*   **Peak Roster (8:00 AM – 11:30 AM)**: Ensure triple-staff coverage (1 POS operator, 2 baristas) to handle the 10:00 AM peak, maintain service velocity, and prevent queue dropouts.
*   **Lean Roster (After 3:00 PM)**: Transition to single-staff coverage to reduce labor overhead during the afternoon lull.
*   **Commuter Alignment**: Shift barista arrival times in Lower Manhattan to **6:45 AM** (to capture the early commuter wave) and in Astoria to **7:30 AM**.

#### II. Marketing & Menu Incentives
*   **Commuter Upsell**: Implement morning POS campaigns to pair coffee orders with Bakery items, increasing average ticket size.
*   **Afternoon Tea Activations**: Promote specialty hot/iced tea offerings between **1:00 PM and 4:00 PM** to stimulate afternoon revenue.

#### III. Store Hours Adjustment
*   Lower Manhattan generates negligible revenue after **6:00 PM** and operates at a loss on weekends. We recommend closing the Lower Manhattan branch at **6:00 PM** on weekdays, and scaling back weekend operating hours. The saved operating expenses can be reinvested into expanding weekend afternoon hours at the Astoria branch, where leisure customer volume remains highly profitable.
